Earth Science Research

Geohazards in a
changing climate.

I study how landslides, debris flows, and other geohazards are responding to climate change in mountain environments — understanding risk, dynamics, and landscape change.

Research themes

Mountains are changing faster than we understand.

My research sits at the intersection of geophysics, geomorphology, hydrology, and climate science — asking how warming temperatures, shifting precipitation patterns, and permafrost thaw are altering the frequency, magnitude, and distribution of mass movements in mountain landscapes.

⛰️

Landslide dynamics

Triggers, runout behavior, and temporal patterns of slope failures across mountain systems.

🌊

Debris flows

How sediment, water, and topography combine to produce channelized mass movements.

🛰️

Remote sensing

Using satellite imagery, lidar, and structure-from-motion to map hazards at scale.

🌡️

Climate linkages

Connecting atmospheric and hydrological drivers to geohazard activity and landscape change.
